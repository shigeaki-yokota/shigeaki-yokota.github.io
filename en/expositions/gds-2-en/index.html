Exposition

GDS II: taking collections of observations to the limit

This explanation is based on the public version arXiv:2603.23325v3. This is an explanation, not the original paper.

The paper is Shigeaki Yokota, Geometry of Geometric Data Set II: Pyramid, arXiv:2603.23325v3, revised March 28, 2026. All theorem numbers and links refer to the text of this version.

A geometric data set (GDS) treats not only the data points but also the functions used to observe them as part of the geometry. If a sequence of data sets does not converge to a single GDS, can we still give it a limit by collecting the observations obtainable from it?

After specifying an approximation condition on feature families, this paper maps a GDS to its collection of dominated observations, a pyramid. These collections form a compact space, adding new limits while preserving convergence in the original observable distance. Moreover, when features can be processed by all real-valued 1-Lipschitz functions on the real line, observable diameter can be recovered from the limit (Theorem 4.10, Proposition 7.3, Corollary 7.7).

Assuming basic metric spaces and probability measures, we follow the definitions and the mechanism of the compactification proof. This is not a reproduction of the entire proof: it explains the reduction to finitely many observations and the operations that prevent observations from being lost in the limit.

Approximate infinitely many features from finitely many representatives

On all GDSs, feature families are too unrestricted for separability in either observable or box distance. The paper therefore fixes a family L\mathcal LL of operations on feature values and selects a class admitting finite approximation (§1).

Why box distance becomes complete and separable

Theorem 4.10. For every monoidal family L\mathcal LL, (D/L,□)(\mathcal D/\mathcal L,\Box)(D/L,□) is complete and separable.

Completeness means that every Cauchy sequence has a limit in this class. Separability means that countably many GDSs suffice to approximate the class to arbitrary precision. The separability proof in Theorem 4.10 replaces finite approximation of features by bounded finite-dimensional data.

Write DM(N,R)\mathcal{DM}(N,R)DM(N,R) for GDSs with at most NNN features, each taking values in [−R,R][-R,R][−R,R]. If FX={f1,…,fN}F_X=\{f_1,\ldots,f_N\}FX​={f1​,…,fN​}, the map

The family L∘DM(N,R)\mathcal L\circ\mathcal{DM}(N,R)L∘DM(N,R) obtained by processing its features is compact too (Definition 4.3, Lemmas 4.5–4.6).

The necessary step is to strengthen approximation in Ky Fan distance to uniform approximation on a common set. Choose a compact K⊂XK\subset XK⊂X carrying most of the probability and apply Lemma 2.8: sufficiently small Ky Fan distance between 1-Lipschitz functions implies small uniform error on KKK.
