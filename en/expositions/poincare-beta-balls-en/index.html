The question is which distance multiplier preserves useful information in measurements as dimension grows. At smaller multipliers, measurements become nearly constant (concentration); at larger ones, sets carrying prescribed probabilities can move far apart (dissipation). The answer has four critical-limit regimes: the shifted-exponential railway pyramid, diameter at most one, a nonlinear Gaussian transform, and a Gaussian limit. The railway regime retains both radial width and angular separation. The other three use a representative shell; in the Gaussian regime a radial Gaussian coordinate must also be retained. The theorem-level completion is the railway upper inclusion, which controls every bounded finite observation, and the sharp criterion for the diameter-at-most-one limit (Introduction, Table 1, Theorems 1.1 and 5.1).

How does this differ from the sphere's Gaussian limit and earlier work?

The sphere's Gaussian pyramid limit is Shioya's theorem, applied at the required normalization in Remark 3.14.
