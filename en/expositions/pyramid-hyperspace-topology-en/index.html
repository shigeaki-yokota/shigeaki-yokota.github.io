Question | Can the weak topology on pyramids be realized as a topology on a space of closed sets?

To study measure concentration, Gromov [3] introduced the viewpoint of replacing an mm-space XXX by the family of all spaces obtained from XXX by a distance-nonincreasing map that preserves the distribution. This family is a pyramid. Shioya [8] added conditions such as Box-closedness to define pyramids, and defined weak convergence (Painlevé–Kuratowski convergence) and the weak topology on the space Π\PiΠ of all pyramids. §1

A pyramid is a subset of the set X\mathcal XX of all mm-spaces. Kazukawa–Nakajima–Shioya [4] considered the hyperspace of nonempty closed subsets of the metric space (X,□)(\mathcal X,\Box)(X,□) (with the Box distance), and asked whether the pyramid space embeds topologically into this hyperspace, for the Fell topology or for the Wijsman topology ([4, Question 9.5]). A pyramid is itself a nonempty closed subset of (X,□)(\mathcal X,\Box)(X,□), so the inclusion map is the natural candidate. The question is whether this inclusion is a topological embedding, that is, whether the weak topology coincides with the topology induced from the hyperspace.

The paper answers in the affirmative, and on a family larger than Π\PiΠ: directedness (one of the conditions defining pyramids) is dropped. The tool is to measure the distance to a family using only spaces with finitely many points. We first define the families and spaces involved.

Obstacle | Without attainment of the infimum, "the family is far from TTT" is not a compact-miss condition
